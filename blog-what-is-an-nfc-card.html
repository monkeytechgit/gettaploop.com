What Is an NFC Card and How Does It Work?

Discover what an NFC card is, how it works with your phone, and why it has become the modern alternative to the paper business card.

NFC in a nutshell

NFC (Near Field Communication) is a short-range wireless technology — just a few centimeters — built into most modern smartphones. It's the same technology behind many contactless payments.

What's inside an NFC card

An NFC card contains a small chip and an antenna. It has no battery — it's powered by your phone's own energy when you tap it nearby. The chip stores a link, and that link opens a digital profile, a website, or a social media page.

How it's used

You hold the card near the back of the other person's phone. The phone reads the chip and opens the link in the browser — no app required. As a backup, TapLoop cards also include a QR code for devices that don't read NFC.

The advantages over paper

You can update your information without reprinting, share far more than a name and phone number (social links, website, portfolio, and more), and present a more modern image. Since the link lives online, changing a detail never requires new cards.

Who is it for?

It's for independent professionals, sales teams, executives, customer-facing businesses, and anyone who meets new people often — at events, trade shows, meetings, or client visits.

FAQ

Do I need an app to use an NFC card?

No. Your phone reads the card and opens the link in your browser.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes, recent models on both platforms can read NFC tags. The card can also include a QR code as a backup.
