Pros and Cons of NFC Cards

An honest look at the pros and cons of NFC business cards: updating your info, cost, compatibility, internet dependency, and more.

In this article
  1. Main advantages
  2. Drawbacks to consider
  3. How to offset the drawbacks
  4. Is it worth it?

Main advantages

  • You share your contact info with a single tap, no app required.
  • Your profile updates without reprinting.
  • It holds far more information than paper ever could.
  • It projects a modern, polished image.
  • Depending on the platform, you can track interactions and capture leads.

Drawbacks to consider

It's not all upside:

  • The person receiving it needs an internet connection to open the profile.
  • Some older phones can't read NFC, which is why it's worth including a QR code.
  • It requires a bigger upfront investment than printing paper cards.
  • If the profile isn't set up well, the card loses its value.

How to offset the drawbacks

Use a card with both NFC and QR, keep your profile short and clear, and test it on different phones. If your audience is more traditional, you can pair it with a printed card as a backup.

Is it worth it?

If you meet new people often or your contact information changes, the investment pays off in convenience and professional image. If you rarely share your details, you may not need one.

FAQ

Does the card need a battery?

No, the chip is powered by the phone's own energy when you tap it nearby.

What happens if there's no internet connection?

The card can still be read, but the profile needs a connection to load.
