Digital vs. Traditional Business Cards

We compare traditional business cards with digital NFC cards: updates, information, cost per use, brand image, and contact tracking.

Paper cards still have their place

Printed cards are familiar and don't depend on technology. But they have clear limits: space is limited, the information goes stale, and many end up forgotten in a drawer.

Information: fixed space vs. unlimited profile

A traditional card fits your name, title, and a few details. A digital NFC card's profile can include your phone number, email, WhatsApp, social media, website, location, catalog, or booking link.

Updates

If you change your number or job title, a printed card becomes outdated. A digital card is updated from the profile, and the same card keeps working.

Brand image

An NFC card in PVC or metal with on-brand design conveys quality and shows that you care about the details. For teams, it keeps a consistent look across the company while giving each team member their own individual profile.

Tracking

With a paper card, you never know if anyone looked at it. With a digital profile, you can collect contacts and measure interactions, depending on the platform you use.

Which one should you choose?

Many people combine both. If you want to stand out, update your info at no extra cost, and connect with a single tap, a digital NFC business card is the most complete option.

FAQ

Does a digital card completely replace a paper one?

It depends on your context. For networking and sales it's usually enough — and more complete — though some businesses keep both.

What if the other person's phone doesn't support NFC?

They can scan the card's QR code and reach the same profile.
