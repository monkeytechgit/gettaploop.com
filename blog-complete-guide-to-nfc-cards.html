What NFC Cards Are and Why They're Growing in the USA

An NFC card is a physical card with a chip and an antenna that opens a link when it's tapped against a compatible phone. For business cards, that link leads to a digital profile with your contact information, social links, and more.

Adoption is growing because it solves everyday problems: paper cards get lost, contact details change, and sharing your information by text or email takes time. With NFC, the exchange happens in seconds.

Types of NFC Business Cards

There are several ways to solve the same need:

  • PVC NFC Card: printed in full color, lightweight, and practical for teams.
  • Metal NFC Card: a premium finish, ideal for executives and corporate gifts.
  • Cards with a built-in QR code: include a visual backup for phones without NFC.

At TapLoop, our cards combine NFC and QR so you're covered in every situation.

How the Process Works, From Purchase to Delivery

The typical flow is: request a quote, choose your material and quantity, send over your logo and details, design and approval, set up your digital profile, test the NFC tap and QR code, and delivery. Having your logo, information, and links ready ahead of time speeds up the process.

What Your Digital Profile Should Include

An effective profile includes your name, title, company, call and WhatsApp buttons, email, professional social links, website, and a button to save your contact. If you're in sales, add a catalog, booking link, or location.

You can find more ideas in our article on what to put on your NFC digital profile.

How to Choose an NFC Card Provider

Before you buy, check these points:

  • They test the NFC tap and the QR code before delivery.
  • The profile can be edited without reprinting the card.
  • You can customize the design with your brand.
  • There are real reviews and past work you can look at.
  • They offer support after delivery.

Common Mistakes When Buying

The most common ones are choosing based on price alone without checking profile quality, not testing the card on both iPhone and Android, overloading the profile with links, and forgetting to include a clear contact button. Another mistake is printing before approving the final design.

Who It Makes the Most Sense For

Independent professionals, sales teams, executives, businesses with face-to-face customer interactions, real estate agencies, law and professional firms, and anyone who attends events regularly. In every case, the value comes from sharing information better and with less friction.
